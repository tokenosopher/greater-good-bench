# Working notes

## Premise
"If everyone read it" is itself the lever. A universally read essay creates
common knowledge. The highest-leverage common knowledge is a correction of
pluralistic ignorance: most people are more decent, moderate, honest and
cooperative than most people think most people are. Correcting that belief,
and making the correction common knowledge, lowers the cost of speaking up,
trusting first, and de-escalating.

## Structure
1. Premise: tell the reader about everyone else, not about themselves
2. The gap: evidence (Princeton drinking, Saudi women working, climate support,
   partisan perception gap, lost wallets, talking to strangers, disaster behaviour)
3. Why we get it wrong: loud sample, silence read as consent, own excuses only, feeds
4. What it costs: norms nobody wants, no first move, self-fulfilling hostility, despair
5. Common knowledge: now everyone knows everyone knows (Asch: one dissenter)
6. Five small things to do
7. Limits: cruel minority, institutions, exploitation
8. Close: the quiet majority is reading this
