# Outline — "Being Wrong, Cheaply"

Thesis: The master variable behind most human improvement is the *cost and speed of
error correction*. Error is the steady state of all minds and institutions. What
separates people, teams, and societies is not how rarely they are wrong but how
cheaply they can find out and change course.

Sections:
1. Scene: the moment of discovering you were wrong — the flinch.
2. Error is the default, not the exception. Perception is construction; memory edits.
3. Why we don't update: beliefs as property/identity; the social tax on public correction.
4. The arithmetic: harm ≈ error × time uncorrected × stakes. Correction rate compounds.
5. Case studies: aviation (CRM, Tenerife), medicine (Semmelweis), science (ideal vs practice),
   forecasting (Tetlock's superforecasters).
6. Practices: pre-mortem; "what would change my mind"; the wrong list; the two-minute apology;
   "you were right"; ask "what am I missing?"; reward correction in public; separate claim from person.
7. Objection: doesn't this make us spineless/relativist? No — firm views, lightly held; confidence ≠ certainty; core commitments.
8. Collective payoff: cheaper correction = less waste, less war, faster learning.
9. Close: the dignity of the updatable person.

Tone: plain, concrete, a few memorable lines. ~3,000 words. No raw HTML. Under 200KB.
