# Brainstorming: What Essay Would Improve the World for the Greatest Number of People?

## 1. Taking the Premise Literally: "If Everyone Read It"

Most essays are written for a self-selected demographic. If *everyone* reads an essay:
1. **Universal Intelligibility:** Its examples must make immediate sense to a nurse in Manila, a farmer in Punjab, a mechanic in Lima, a teacher in Nairobi, a software engineer in Berlin, and a parent in Ohio. It must draw from universal human realities: water, fever, bread, argument, shame, silence, work, children, tools, debt, promises, strangers, and time.
2. **Perceptual Triggers Over Abstract Virtues:** Abstract exhortations ("be kind," "think critically," "cooperate") fail on Tuesday morning because nobody believes they are being cruel or irrational—people cause harm when they feel cornered, exhausted, insulted, frightened, or certain they are right. An essay only changes behavior if it installs **perceptual triggers**—moments where an automatic sensation (righteous certainty, retaliatory heat, crowd silence, midnight despair) becomes the cue to pause and execute a specific counter-move.
3. **Exploiting Common Knowledge:** The unique power of *everyone* reading the same text is not merely eight billion isolated self-improvements; it is **coordination**. Many of humanity's worst failures are multiperson traps—escalation spirals, pluralistic ignorance, bystander paralysis, scapegoating, and institutional decay—where no single person can easily step down alone because stepping down is misread by others. When everyone has read the same field guide to human traps, naming the trap out loud turns private doubt into **common knowledge** and breaks the spell.

## 2. Five Concentric Circles of Preventable Suffering

1. **The Body in Trouble (When Folk Intuition Kills):**
   - Oral Rehydration Therapy (`1 liter clean water + 6 level teaspoons sugar + 1/2 level teaspoon salt`) vs. the fatal folk instinct to withhold water from a child with diarrhea.
   - Burns (cool running water for 20 minutes vs. butter/oil/ice), bleeding (10 minutes uninterrupted direct pressure vs. peeking), choking (back blows and thrusts vs. blind finger sweeps), suicide crises (asking directly and buying time past the transient neurological storm vs. the myth that asking plants the idea).
   - Invisible contagion (Semmelweis, soap, ventilation, antibiotic stewardship).
   - The metabolic brain (sleep deprivation, amygdala reactivity, HALT).

2. **The Mind in Error (How to Hold a Map Without Worshiping a Uniform):**
   - Kathryn Schulz & Robert Burton on the feeling of certainty vs. the sting of correction.
   - Beliefs as **maps** (tools for navigation) vs. **uniforms** (tribal loyalty badges), and the Falsification Test.
   - The Sunk-Cost Trap.
   - Abraham Wald's bombers and the **Silent Graveyard** (survivorship bias in medicine, gambling, and fear).
   - Anatol Rapoport's **Steel Mirror** for disagreement.

3. **The Five-Meter Radius (Stopping the Escalation Engine):**
   - The Asymmetric Lens (fundamental attribution error) and the Mind-Reading Trap.
   - Shergill's 2003 *Science* experiment on sensory attenuation: why "an eye for an eye" escalates physical force by 38% per turn, and why fair-minded people need the **Twenty-Percent Discount**.
   - Why humiliation works for ten minutes and fails for ten years.
   - **Witness Before Wrench** and the three-sentence **Clean Repair** (with zero *buts*).

4. **The Spell of the Crowd (Traps That Require Common Knowledge):**
   - The Roadside Trap (diffusion of responsibility) and pointing to *"You in the blue shirt."*
   - The Emperor's Court (pluralistic ignorance) and the **Naive Question**.
   - The Outrage Merchant's Mirror (the Worst-One-Percent trick).
   - Three laws of living institutions: **Chesterton's Fence**, **Goodhart's Target**, and **Ostrom's Well**.

5. **The Invisible Ledger (Trust, Compounding, and the Long Relay):**
   - Zero-sum slices vs. positive-sum recipes.
   - High-trust commons built one kept promise at a time.
   - Exponential compounding: the moneylender's trap vs. the craftsman's daily twenty minutes.
   - The Glory of the Maintainer and the **One-Way Mirror** (how we treat those with zero leverage over us, including future generations).
